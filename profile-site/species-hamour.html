Species in motion

Hamour, from hatchery to sea

Scientific & common names

  • Hamour — local name
  • Orange-spotted Grouper — common name
  • Epinephelus coioides — scientific name

Biological characteristics

A high-value species with firm texture, strong market pricing and cultural importance across the GCC and Asia.

Natural habitat & distribution

Native to regional waters. Found across the Indo-West Pacific, including the Arabian Gulf, around coastal reefs, estuaries and mangrove areas.

Environmental tolerances

As a regional native it tolerates local salinity and temperature ranges well, reducing biological risk for downstream farms.

Commercial farming characteristics

Commonly stocked in marine cage systems. A slow-growing species with premium market value.

Production methodology

Fry are produced through controlled broodstock, larval and nursery phases — supporting consistent size grading, stable early feeding and smooth transfer into nursery tanks or sea cages.

Fry availability & specifications

  • Pilot facility (2024): annual capacity of 2 million larvae and fry
  • First sale of Hamour fry to the local market in 2024
  • Grouper juveniles supplied to a local company since 2024 — projected 7 million/year by 2026 (with Red Snapper)
  • Indonesia hatchery with Aquagrow International: 300,000 fry/month, launching 2026
